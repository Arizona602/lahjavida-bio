Dye-Drug Conjugate platform

Advancing cancer therapy designed to reach tumors, not healthy tissue.

Lahjavida's Dye-Drug Conjugates (DDCs) pair a near-infrared dye with proven chemotherapy, designed so the drug concentrates in the tumor rather than spreading through the body, with the dye lighting the tumor up for imaging.

The problem

Chemotherapy works. It just can't tell the difference.

Chemotherapy is still a backbone of cancer care, but it travels everywhere the blood goes. Healthy cells take the hit, and toxicity limits the dose.

  • Everywhere at onceThe drug spreads through the whole body. Only a small share of it reaches the tumor.
  • Healthy cells payBlood, gut, hair, and nerve cells are damaged, causing the side effects patients fear most.
  • The dose gets cappedToxicity, not effectiveness, often sets the limit, so tumors get less drug than they could.

Illustrative concept, not study data. ● tumor  ○ healthy tissue

The insight

Tumors pull our dye in. Healthy tissue mostly keeps it out.

How the dye concentrates in tumors is still under investigation; a leading hypothesis is that many solid tumors over-express OATP transporters that draw in this class of near-infrared dye. Lahjavida's platform started in 2009 as an imaging discovery. The company then turned the imaging dye into a delivery vehicle by attaching chemotherapy to it.

Independent research on the same mechanism: heptamethine carbocyanine dye uptake via the HIF-1α/OATP axis in canine and human cancers ↗

The platform

One molecule. Three jobs.

A DDC is a small molecule built from three parts. Each part solves a problem that standard chemotherapy can't.

01 · Homing + beacon

Near-infrared dye

It concentrates in tumors and glows in near-infrared light. That makes it both the guidance system and the flashlight.

02 · Safety switch

Cleavable linker

It keeps the drug locked and inactive in the bloodstream, then breaks apart inside the tumor to release it.

03 · Proven payload

Standard chemotherapy

It uses well-understood cancer drugs. The lead program carries MMAE, the payload used in several FDA-approved antibody-drug conjugates.

    Lahjavida Bio03 · The insight
    Tumors pull our dye in. Healthy tissue mostly keeps it out.
    Mechanism

    One leading idea links uptake to OATP transporters over-expressed in many tumors (tied to tumor hypoxia, HIF-1α). In testing, raising or lowering OATP did not change uptake, so several routes remain under investigation.

    Origin

    The platform began in 2009 as an imaging discovery. Lahjavida turned the imaging dye into a delivery vehicle.

    Independent support

    Peer-reviewed research has described OATP-related uptake of this dye class; Lahjavida is still testing which routes drive DDC6 uptake.

    Here's our edge. Tumors are metabolically hungry, and many of them make extra transporters called OATPs. Our near-infrared dye gets pulled in through those transporters, while healthy tissue mostly keeps it out. This started as an imaging discovery in 2009, and independent peer-reviewed research describes the same uptake mechanism for this class of dye. We turned the imaging dye into a delivery vehicle.

    Lahjavida Bio05 · How it works
    From bloodstream to inside the cancer cell.
    01 · Circulate

    Switched off

    The linker keeps the chemotherapy inactive as it travels through the blood.

    02 · Target

    Tumors pull it in

    The dye concentrates in tumor tissue; healthy cells mostly pass it by. Uptake route under study.

    03 · Release

    Drug unlocked

    The linker breaks inside the tumor, freeing the drug to attack the cancer cell from within.

    04 · Illuminate

    See where it went

    The near-infrared glow passes through tissue, so the tumor becomes visible.

    Live animation available on the website
    Walk through the four steps. One: it circulates switched off. Two: tumors pull it in through OATP transporters. Three: the linker breaks inside the tumor and releases the drug where it's needed. Four: because the dye glows in near-infrared, you can actually see where the drug went. If you're presenting live, this is a good moment to show the animation on the website.
